## **1\. Session Content Breakdown & Question Framework**

### **Foundation Sessions Overview**

The micro-session architecture transforms the current single questionnaire into three sequential coaching conversations, each designed to achieve specific positioning breakthroughs based on proven methodology that has achieved 100% completion rates.

### **Session 1: Functional Identity Lock-In (7-9 minutes)**

**Session Purpose:** Force users to identify their primary functional positioning within the five-function framework that VCs and hiring managers actually use for decision-making, establishing the foundation for credibility assessment.

**Core Methodology Framework:** The session employs the "Five Functions Reality Check" \- forcing users to choose their primary business function before they can articulate specific credibility within that function.

**The Five Functions Framework:** Every company, regardless of size or stage, has five core functions. Everything ladders up to these five that sit with the CEO at the decision-making table. Users must choose their primary functional identity before they can establish credibility:

◦ **Finance**: Strategic resource allocation, financial planning, investor relations, budget optimization, ROI analysis ◦ **Operations**: Process optimization, service delivery, organizational efficiency, supply chain, vendor management  
◦ **Sales**: Revenue generation, relationship building, client acquisition, business development, partnerships ◦ **Marketing**: Brand development, positioning, market strategy, communications, competitive analysis, demand generation ◦ **Product**: Solution development, user experience, feature prioritization, technical architecture, development roadmaps

**Opening Framework Examples:** ◦ "There are five functions that create business value: Finance, Operations, Sales, Marketing, Product. Your expertise lives primarily in ONE. Which one claims 80% of your credible value?" ◦ "Before we talk about what you've accomplished, let's identify which functional bucket your expertise lives in." ◦ "Every company has these five functions. You need to pick your lane before you can prove your credibility."

**Challenge Patterns for Vague Aspirational Statements:**

*When Users Say "Lead Strategy":* ◦ "Strategy isn't a function \- it's an approach. Is this financial strategy, marketing strategy, or operational strategy?" ◦ "Everyone thinks they do strategy. What function OWNS the strategy you want to lead?" ◦ "There's Finance strategy, Operations strategy, Marketing strategy, Sales strategy, Product strategy. Pick your lane."

*When Users Say "Impact the Business":* ◦ "That could mean anything. Do you impact business through: driving revenue (Sales), reducing costs (Operations), allocating resources (Finance), shaping perception (Marketing), or deciding what gets built (Product)?" ◦ "Everyone impacts the business. HOW specifically do you create business value?" ◦ "Impact through which function? What's your mechanism of impact?"

*When Users Say "Influence Products":* ◦ "Are you the one deciding what gets built (Product function), or are you marketing what gets built (Marketing function), or are you selling what gets built (Sales function)?" ◦ "Influence how \- through market research, user feedback, competitive analysis, technical specifications, or go-to-market strategy?" ◦ "Product people decide what gets built. Marketing people position what gets built. Sales people sell what gets built. Which one are you?"

**Anti-Generalist Enforcement Patterns:**

*The Small Company vs. Big Company Test:* ◦ "Everyone does 'everything' at small companies. What would you do at a specialized role at a large company?" ◦ "You're describing what you had to do, not what you're best at. What would you CHOOSE to own at a bigger company?"

*The 80/20 Forcing Framework:* ◦ "You mentioned Finance AND Operations AND Marketing. Which function do you OWN versus merely participate in?" ◦ "Primary function gets 80%, secondary gets 20%. Nothing else matters for positioning. What's your 80%?" ◦ "Cross-functional collaboration is great. Cross-functional positioning is career suicide. Pick your lane."

**Function-Specific Validation Questions:**

*For Operations Claims:* ◦ "Operations makes things work better, faster, cheaper. What processes have you personally optimized or built?" ◦ "When you say 'drive transformation,' do you mean you redesigned how work gets done?" ◦ "What systems do people depend on that you created or improved?"

*For Marketing Claims:* ◦ "Marketing drives demand and shapes perception. What market positioning or messaging did you create?" ◦ "When you say 'strategic impact,' do you mean you changed how the market perceives something?" ◦ "What campaigns or initiatives did you lead that moved business metrics?"

*For Finance Claims:* ◦ "Finance people think in ROI, budgets, resource allocation. What financial decisions have you owned?" ◦ "When you say 'business impact,' do you mean you influenced how money gets allocated or spent?" ◦ "What budget or financial process do you personally manage or optimize?"

*For Sales Claims:* ◦ "Sales generates revenue through relationships and deals. What revenue have you directly influenced or generated?" ◦ "When you say 'business development,' do you mean you personally closed deals or enabled others to close deals?" ◦ "What client relationships or revenue streams do you own or have you built?"

*For Product Claims:* ◦ "Product determines what gets built and why. What have you built that users depend on?" ◦ "When you say 'innovation,' do you mean you decided what features/products to build?" ◦ "What product decisions have you made that affected user experience or business outcomes?"

**Session 1 Success Criteria:** ◦ **Function Selection**: User chooses one primary function from the five-function framework ◦ **80/20 Clarity**: Establishes clear functional hierarchy (80% primary, 20% supporting capabilities) ◦ **Anti-Generalist Commitment**: Stops claiming equal expertise across multiple functions ◦ **Functional Language**: Uses terminology specific to their chosen function rather than generic business language
