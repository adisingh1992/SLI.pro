# Wing Approachable Micro-Journaling Prompt Library
## 100+ Prompts Per Audience · August 2026

---

## How This Library Works

This library is the warm, approachable layer of Wing's micro-journaling system. It sits alongside (and should be weighted more heavily than) the serious/reflective prompt libraries already built. The design principle: the user should feel like they're chatting with a smart friend who's curious about their life, not filling out a professional development exercise. Underneath every question, Wing is capturing behavioral signal — but the user just thinks they're having a good time.

**Rotation weighting:** Roughly 2:1 approachable-to-serious in any given week. Two of these for every one serious/reflective prompt.

**Voice standard:** Wing during ongoing engagement leads with affirming and encouraging. Warm and playful without being immature. Smart friend, not cartoon mascot. Contractions always. Specific over abstract. No hedging, no therapy-speak, no corporate career-center language.

**Event sensitivity rule:** Never assume the user attended an event they haven't mentioned. No "How was the career fair?" or "How'd the networking event go?" unless Wing already knows they went. Use general scenes instead — weekends, conversations, breaks, commutes, dinners, group chats.

---

## Universal Openers

Every prompt is preceded by a greeting + opener. The greeting uses the user's name. The opener rotates from this pool:

**Casual check-in openers:**
○ "Hey [Name] — quick one for you."
○ "Hey [Name] — been thinking about this."
○ "Hi [Name] — curious about something."
○ "Hey [Name] — OK real talk."
○ "Hi [Name] — random one today."
○ "[Name]! Got one for you."
○ "Hey [Name] — this one's fun."
○ "Hi [Name] — one thing I want to ask you."

**Warm-return openers (for users who haven't responded in 5+ days):**
○ "Hey [Name] — missed you. Quick question."
○ "Hi [Name] — just one today, promise."
○ "[Name]! Your Wing wants to hear what's up."

**Post-response openers (when Wing is following up on a theme):**
○ "Hey [Name] — something you said last time stuck with me."
○ "Hi [Name] — following up on something."
○ "[Name] — remember what you told me about [topic]? Related question."

### How You Show Up

1. "How did you introduce yourself to someone recently — at a party, a meeting, anywhere? What did you actually say?"
*Signal: default self-description vs. SLI language. Tracks internalization over time.*

2. "Did anyone ask you what you do this week? How'd that conversation go?"
*Signal: positioning comfort level. Whether the description was fluid or fumbled.*

3. "What did you talk about at lunch or on a break with someone this week?"
*Signal: organic topic ownership. What people discuss casually reveals what they're actually thinking about.*

4. "Last time someone new asked about your work, did you feel like you nailed it or like you're still figuring out the right words?"
*Signal: self-assessed positioning clarity.*

5. "Have you caught yourself describing your work differently to different people lately? What changed?"
*Signal: contextual adaptation — conscious or unconscious narrative divergence.*

6. "If a friend introduced you to someone at a party, what would they say about you? Would you agree with it?"
*Signal: external perception awareness vs. self-model.*

7. "What's something you said about your work this week that felt really right — like, that's exactly how I want to explain this?"
*Signal: positioning breakthrough moments. Captures language that's working.*

8. "Who did you spend the most time with this week — and did any of it surprise you?"
*Signal: network attention allocation and social expectations.*

### What's On Your Mind

9. "What shaped your thinking this week? A podcast, a conversation, something you read, a random thought in the shower — anything."

10. "What's the most interesting thing someone said to you this week?"

*Signal: unresolved processing. What occupies mental bandwidth.*

12. "What did you spend an unreasonable amount of time thinking about this week that has nothing to do with work?"
*Signal: off-lane interests. The non-work version reveals what genuinely captures their curiosity.*

13. "Is there something you overheard or read recently that you keep coming back to?"
*Signal: sticky information — what lodges in memory reveals values and priorities.*

14. "What topic have you been wanting to talk to someone about but haven't found the right person?"

15. "Did anything make you laugh at work this week? What was it?"

16. "What's one thing you learned this week — big or small, doesn't matter?"

### The Hypotheticals

61. "If you had to quit your job tomorrow and couldn't do anything even adjacent to what you do now — what would you try first?"
*Signal: latent identity. The non-adjacent aspiration reveals what they'd pursue without constraint.*

62. "If someone shadowed you for a full workday, what would they be most confused by?"

*Signal: priorities through scarcity. What gets kept reveals what actually matters.*

65. "If you could go back and whisper one sentence to yourself on your first day at your current job, what are you saying?"

66. "If someone made a documentary about your last month, is it a thriller, a comedy, a slow-burn drama, or a nature documentary?"
*Signal: self-narrative genre. How they characterize their current chapter.*

67. "If you could only communicate through voice memos for a week, how would your work change?"
*Signal: communication preferences and relationship to written vs. spoken expression.*

68. "If you had to teach a class on something you learned the hard way, what's the course called?"
*Signal: earned expertise. The "hard way" filter surfaces real experience, not credentials.*

## Integration Notes

○ This library replaces the need for separate "fun" or "playful" prompt categories. Instead, it IS the primary prompt rotation, with the serious/reflective libraries weighted as the less-frequent complement.
○ Every prompt in this library should be tagged with its behavioral signal in the prompt_library schema so the adaptive selection engine can choose based on what signal is most informative for the current user.
○ The universal opener pool should be implemented as a separate layer — the system selects the opener independently of the prompt, ensuring variety in how prompts feel even when the underlying question repeats at the 14-day minimum interval.
○ The event-sensitivity rule is a constraint on the adaptive selection engine: prompts referencing specific events (conferences, interviews, career fairs, networking events) are only eligible for delivery if the user has mentioned the event in a prior response or through a detected calendar signal.
